Same gift to the charity. Less tax.

Your shares haven't grown, so there's no capital gains advantage here. In fact, if they've dropped, selling first may let you claim the loss. That's a conversation worth having.

When you donate publicly traded shares directly to a registered charity, the capital gain is taxed at a rate of zero. Sell them first and give the cash, and half the gain is taxed at your marginal rate. Same generosity, different bill.
